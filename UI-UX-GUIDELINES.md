# Arvello UI/UX Guidelines

## Product Experience

Arvello is a premium furniture-commerce experience.

The UI should communicate:
- quality;
- comfort;
- trust;
- craftsmanship;
- modern home styling;
- easy product discovery;
- low-friction shopping.

The interface should feel refined and spacious rather than aggressive or overly technical.

## Layout Language

The visual reference uses:
- white/warm-neutral page surfaces;
- dark green brand sections;
- gold accent actions and highlights;
- large lifestyle photography;
- rounded product cards;
- restrained borders;
- soft shadows;
- clear section headings;
- compact product metadata;
- generous vertical whitespace.

## Page Rhythm

Use a predictable rhythm:

1. Header/navigation
2. Hero or page introduction
3. Trust/benefit strip when relevant
4. Main discovery/content section
5. Supporting promotional section
6. Social proof or editorial content
7. Newsletter/CTA
8. Footer

Do not force every page to use every section.

## Header

The header should remain simple and commerce-focused.

Typical hierarchy:
- brand/logo;
- primary navigation;
- search;
- account;
- cart.

Desktop navigation can expose more links. Mobile should collapse secondary navigation into an accessible menu.

## Hero

Hero sections should contain:
- concise eyebrow/overline;
- strong headline;
- supporting copy;
- one primary CTA;
- optional secondary action;
- relevant lifestyle image.

Avoid excessive text over photography unless contrast is excellent.

## Product Cards

Product cards should consistently support:
- image;
- product name;
- rating/review count where available;
- current price;
- previous price when discounted;
- sale badge when relevant;
- cart/wishlist action when supported.

Keep card internals aligned so a grid does not visually jump from card to card.

## Product Imagery

Furniture imagery is a major part of the brand.

Rules:
- use consistent aspect ratios within a collection;
- use object-fit behavior intentionally;
- avoid awkward crops;
- preserve product visibility;
- use lifestyle imagery for inspiration and clean product imagery for catalog cards;
- provide meaningful alt text.

## Buttons

Primary CTA:
- dark green background;
- white/inverse text;
- medium rounded corners;
- clear hover and active state.

Accent CTA:
- gold background;
- dark text;
- use for promotional emphasis.

Secondary CTA:
- white/surface background;
- subtle border;
- green text.

Do not create additional button colors without a design-system reason.

## Cards

Cards should feel light and premium.

Preferred:
- white surface;
- subtle neutral border;
- 8–12px radius;
- restrained shadow when elevation is necessary.

Avoid:
- thick borders;
- strong black shadows;
- excessive blur;
- multiple competing accent colors.

## Section Headings

Use a consistent heading pattern:
- title;
- optional short subtitle;
- optional "View All" action.

Keep the title visually dominant but not oversized relative to the page.

## Trust & Social Proof

Trust elements should be visually quiet and easy to scan.

Examples:
- free shipping;
- easy returns;
- secure payment;
- support;
- ratings;
- customer testimonials.

Do not overuse badges.

## Promotional Sections

Promotional banners can use the dark green surface with gold accents and furniture lifestyle photography.

Promotion hierarchy:
1. offer;
2. supporting benefit;
3. CTA;
4. image/product context.

## Navigation & Interaction

Every interactive element must have:
- clear affordance;
- visible hover state;
- visible keyboard focus;
- disabled state where relevant;
- sensible active state.

## UX Principle

Prefer clarity over decoration.

Every visual element should answer at least one question:
- What is this?
- Why does it matter?
- What can I do next?
- Where am I?
